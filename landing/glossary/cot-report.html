What Is the COT Report?

Commitment of Traders · Positioning data

Who publishes it, and when

The Commitment of Traders (COT) report is released every Friday at 3:30pm ET by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC). It breaks down that week's open interest — as of the prior Tuesday — for every futures market where enough large traders hold reportable positions.

It's the closest thing the futures market has to an X-ray: instead of just price, you see who actually holds the open contracts behind that price.

The trader categories

  • Commercials: producers, processors, and merchants hedging real physical exposure — an airline hedging jet fuel, a farmer hedging next season's corn. Their positioning reflects the physical business, not a market call.
  • Non-commercials: large speculators — hedge funds and CTAs — trading the contract for a view, not to hedge an underlying business.
  • Non-reportable positions: everyone below the CFTC's reporting threshold, mostly smaller retail and individual traders, lumped together as a residual.

Why traders watch positioning

Extremes matter more than the raw number. When non-commercial length in a market reaches a multi-year high, it means the obvious bullish case is already crowded — most of the money that wanted in is already in, which is exactly the setup that leaves a market vulnerable to a sharp reversal if the story changes. The COT report is how you see that crowding forming before it shows up as a violent move in price.

It's a sentiment and positioning gauge, not a timing signal — the report is a week old by the time it's published, and crowded positioning can stay crowded for a long time before it unwinds. Most traders use it alongside price action and the futures curve, not on its own.
