This is the tactical build-out of **Engine 02 (embed distribution)** for one channel.

> **2026-09-11: retail traders only.** ICP D (procurement/physical-side/B2B) is scrapped as a target
> segment across this doc — see `GROWTH_PLAN_2026H2.md`. This channel now exists to reach ICP A

## Why LinkedIn, and why creators

LinkedIn is where **ICP B** (analysts, newsletter writers) actually are, alongside plenty of ICP A
retail traders who follow them. Neither responds to advertising. Both respond to a peer they already
trust citing a number.

The channel logic follows the Benzinga lesson: **distribute the data through other people's audiences
rather than buying your own.** On LinkedIn specifically that is not sponsored posts. It is co-creation
and citation. The currency is being genuinely useful to someone whose professional reputation depends
on having good charts.

**The structural insight:** every commodity analyst on LinkedIn needs charts, every week, on a
schedule. Today they build them in Excel, screenshot a terminal they often cannot legally
redistribute, or go without. We already produce exactly the charts they need — forward curves, COT
positioning, seasonality — for markets nobody else charts well. That gap is the whole opening.

---

## The centrepiece: own the release calendar

**This is the highest-leverage idea in this document.** Commodities differ from equities in that the
news is *scheduled*. There is a predictable weekly spike of commodity content on LinkedIn, pinned to
releases we already track on `/calendar`:

| Release | Cadence | The post everyone wants to make |
|---|---|---|
| EIA Weekly Petroleum Status | Wednesdays | Crude/product draw or build vs expectation |
| EIA Natural Gas Storage | Thursdays | Injection/withdrawal vs 5-yr average |
| CFTC Commitment of Traders | Fridays | What managed money did this week |
| WASDE | Monthly | Supply/demand revision, corn/wheat/soy |
| OPEC+ meetings | Periodic | Quota decision and curve reaction |

**The program:** within ~30–60 minutes of each release, produce the clean, branded chart and send it
— unasked, free, no strings — to the creators covering that market. They get a publish-ready asset at
the exact moment they need it. Every placement carries attribution.

This is systematic, repeatable, requires no negotiation, and nobody is doing it for commodities. It
turns a one-off outreach problem into a recurring service that compounds. Start with Friday COT — it
is the release with the most commentary and the least good free charting.

## The funnel

```
STAGE 0  Foundation      Make yourself pitchable
STAGE 1  Sourcing        Find and tier the voices
STAGE 2  Warm-up         Earn the right to ask
STAGE 3  The give        Asymmetric offer, no ask attached
STAGE 4  Collaboration   Six formats, ranked
STAGE 5  Capture         Where their audience lands
STAGE 6  Conversion      Free tool → account → paid
STAGE 7  Measure         Per-creator attribution
```

### Stage 0 — Foundation (weeks 1–2)

You cannot pitch creators from an empty profile. Before any outreach:

- **Founder profile**, not just a company page. On LinkedIn, people follow people. The company page is
  a footnote; your personal profile is the asset.
- Headline states the category claim: *building the analytics platform for commodities — curves, CFTC
  positioning, seasonality*.
- **Post 12–15 times first.** Publish the charts you intend to give away, for three weeks, before
  asking anyone for anything. This proves the goods exist and gives creators something to check.
- Featured section: the roll-yield calculator, the calendar, one `/now` page. Free tools, no signup.
- Company page exists and is linked, but expect ~5% of the reach of your personal profile.

**Gate:** you have a visible body of work a stranger can evaluate in 30 seconds.

### Stage 1 — Sourcing and tiering (week 3)

Build a list of ~120 names. **Tier by audience fit and engagement rate, not follower count.**

**Archetypes to fill:**

| Archetype | Why they matter | Which ICP they reach |
|---|---|---|
| Energy analysts (crude, refining, crack spreads, LNG) | Highest posting volume; EIA releases are their beat | A, B |
| Ag economists & extension faculty | Deeply trusted, WASDE-driven, chronically under-tooled | B |
| Metals & mining analysts | Underserved by free charting; COT-curious | A, B |
| Freight & dry bulk analysts | Adjacent, high engagement, curve-literate | B |
| Ex-trading-house professionals (Glencore/Trafigura/Cargill alumni) | Credibility halo; small but potent audiences | B |
| Trade press journalists | Not creators, but citation multipliers | all |

**How to find them (do this, don't guess at names):**

- Hashtag sweeps: `#commodities` `#oilandgas` `#agriculture` `#WASDE` `#naturalgas` `#supplychain`
  `#metals` `#freight` `#commoditytrading`
- Search LinkedIn for the release names themselves — `"WASDE"`, `"COT report"`, `"EIA storage"` —
  sorted by recent. This surfaces the people who post *on the calendar*, which is exactly the cohort
  the centrepiece program serves.
- Comment sections of the biggest posts in each hashtag: the engaged mid-tier voices are usually in
  the replies, not the byline.
- Cross-reference against Substack and X — an analyst with a newsletter is an embed-widget target
  (Stage 4, format 3), which is worth far more than a single post.

**Tiers:**

- **Micro (1k–15k, high engagement):** ~80 of your 120. Highest yes-rate, best conversion per
  impression, most likely to become long-term partners. **Start here.**
- **Mid (15k–100k):** ~30. Worth real effort, expect months of warm-up.
- **Anchor (100k+):** ~10. Do not pitch these until the program has proof. One anchor citation is
  worth more after 20 micro placements than before.

### Stage 2 — Warm-up (weeks 3–6, runs continuously)

Cold pitching on LinkedIn has a near-zero yes-rate and burns the name permanently. Instead, for 3–4
weeks per target:

- Comment substantively on their posts — add a data point, not praise. A comment that contributes a
  number is itself a demonstration of the product.
- Never link in a first comment on someone else's post. It reads as hijacking and suppresses their reach.
- Share their post with your own added chart, tagging them. This is the single strongest warm-up move:
  it gives them reach, and shows exactly what collaborating would look like.

**Gate:** they have engaged with you at least twice before any DM.

### Stage 3 — The give (asymmetric offer, no ask attached)

The first DM contains **no request**. It contains a gift already delivered.

> *"You've been covering the diesel crack all month — I built you the 5-year seasonal for it, plus
> the current curve. Yours to use, credited or not, no strings. If it's useful I'll send the updated
> one each Wednesday after the EIA print."*

That last sentence is the whole funnel. It converts a one-time gift into a standing relationship, and
it is the on-ramp to the release-calendar program.

**The compensation ladder** — what you can offer, cheapest first. Most creators in this niche are
motivated by credibility and better material, not fees:

| Offer | Your cost | Their perceived value |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime Pro account | ~$0 marginal | Moderate |
| Custom chart / data pull on request | Time | **Very high** |
| Standing post-release chart service | Time, batched | **Very high** |
| Co-branded report with their name on it | Time | High — it is a portfolio piece |
| Raised-limit API key | ~$0 marginal | High for the quant-leaning |
| Feature in Daily Brief + cross-post | ~$0 | Moderate — reciprocal reach |
| Revenue share on referred subscriptions | Revenue | Moderate — **see blocker below** |
| Cash fee | Cash | Usually unnecessary here |

> **Blocked:** subscription revenue share needs referral tracking and promo codes that do not exist in
> RevenueCat today. Do not promise it until it is built. The affiliate infrastructure in
> `src/config/affiliates.ts` is outbound-to-broker, not inbound-creator — it is not the same thing.

### Stage 4 — Collaboration formats, ranked

| # | Format | Effort | Reach | Conv. | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Chart citation** ("data via Commodity Hub") | S | High | Low–med | Volume play. The base of the pyramid — aim for many, weekly. |
| 2 | **Document carousel co-analysis** | M | **High** | Med | LinkedIn pushes native PDF documents hard. Chart-heavy commodity content is ideal for it. |
| 3 | **Newsletter embed widget** | S | Med | **High** | The Benzinga move. Recurring, compounding, and a backlink. Highest long-term value per placement. |
| 4 | **Co-branded quarterly report** | L | Med | **High** | e.g. *"Q4 Natural Gas Positioning Report"* with their name. Best trust asset you can manufacture. |
| 5 | **LinkedIn Live / audio around a release** | M | Med | Med | Pin to WASDE or an OPEC+ meeting. Their audience, your data, live. |
| 6 | **Guest slot in Daily Brief, cross-posted** | S | Low | Med | Reciprocity engine — cheapest way to keep a relationship warm between bigger collaborations. |

**Run 1 and 3 continuously. Use 2 and 4 as the quarterly set-pieces. 5 and 6 are relationship
maintenance.**

### Stage 5 — Capture: where their audience lands

**Never send LinkedIn traffic to a paywall.** This audience is in discovery mode, not buying mode, and
a pricing page kills the referral and embarrasses the creator who sent them.

Land them, in order of preference:

1. The specific `/now/<commodity>` or `/commodities/<commodity>` page the chart came from — no signup
2. A free tool: roll-yield calculator, `/calendar`, `/embed`
3. Only then a free account

**Two LinkedIn mechanics that matter:**

- Outbound links in the post body suppress reach. Put the link in the first comment, or use the
  "comment *CURVE* and I'll send it" mechanic — which also generates the comment volume the algorithm
  rewards, and gives the creator a list.
- Every collaboration gets its own UTM (`utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=creator&utm_campaign=<handle>`)
  so Stage 7 works at all.

### Stage 6 — Conversion path

```
Creator post → free page (no signup) → free tool → free account
   → alert / watchlist created  ← the activation moment
   → Premium $6.99  → Pro $19.99
```

The activation event is unchanged from `CONVERSION_PIPELINE.md`: first alert, first pinned market, or
first API call. LinkedIn's contribution is filling the top, not shortcutting the middle.

**Expect a lag.** Even an individual retail trader referred by a trusted creator typically evaluates
for a while before subscribing. Judge this channel on 90-day cohorts, not week-one conversion.

### Stage 7 — Measurement

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Placements per month, by format | Program throughput |
| Referred sessions per creator | Which relationships are real |
| Signups per creator, per format | Which *formats* work, not just which people |
| Live embeds from LinkedIn-sourced creators | Compounding asset count |
| **Unprompted citations** | The flywheel indicator — mentions you did not ask for |
| 90-day cohort conversion to paid | The only revenue number that means anything here |

**Prerequisite:** confirm PostHog is actually receiving funnel events before trusting any of this.
`CONVERSION_PIPELINE.md` flags that these events previously sat in a client-side queue.

## 90-day operating plan

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| **1–2** | Stage 0. Profile rewrite, 12–15 posts, featured tools. No outreach at all. |
| **3–4** | Build the 120-name list. Begin warm-up on the 80 micro-tier. Start posting the Friday COT chart yourself. |
| **5–6** | First 25 gives (Stage 3). Launch the release-calendar chart service to whoever accepts. |
| **7–9** | First carousel collaborations and newsletter embeds. Aim: 10 placements, 5 live embeds. |
| **10–12** | First co-branded quarterly report. Approach 3 mid-tier voices with the proof from weeks 7–9. |
| **13** | Review by format, not by person. Kill the two weakest formats; double the best. |

**Realistic throughput for one person:** 4–8 placements/month by week 12. This is a compounding
channel, not a launch channel.

## Risks and constraints

- **Disclosure is mandatory.** Any creator receiving free Pro, custom work, or revenue share has a
  material connection and must disclose it. Give them the disclosure line pre-written. Undisclosed
  promotion is both a legal problem and, on a professional network, a reputational one for them —
  which makes it your problem too.
- **Keep the "not advice" line intact.** The marketing plan treats *not a broker, not investment
  advice* as a trust asset. Brief every collaborator on it. A creator implying performance claims on
  your data is the fastest way to lose their trust, and yours.
- **The CFD affiliate is in direct tension with this channel.** eToro CTAs monetise ICP A, but ICP B
  reads CFD promotion as a reason to discount your analysis — and eToro's own compliance rules
  restrict promotional copy anyway. **Recommendation:** suppress affiliate CTAs for LinkedIn-referred
  sessions. That is a code change; it does not exist today.
- **Don't over-index on one anchor voice.** A single large creator driving most referrals is a
  concentration risk that disappears the moment they lose interest.
- **LinkedIn suppresses outbound links.** Design every collaboration around that constraint from the
  start rather than discovering it in the numbers.
- **This is manual work with no shortcut.** Same honest caveat as Engine 02 in the growth plan: it is
  outreach, follow-ups, and chart production, and it will feel slow before it compounds.
