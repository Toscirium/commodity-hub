## What is and isn't verified here

**Verified:** every person below publishes publicly under that name, at the publication named, on
commodity or commodity-adjacent markets. Each was found through search or a fetched page, not recalled.

**NOT verified — you must confirm before outreach:**

- LinkedIn presence, handle, or follower count. LinkedIn is auth-walled and cannot be checked from here.
  The funnel tiers by engagement rate anyway, which only you can see.
- Whether they are currently active or posting.
- Whether they would be receptive.

**Treat this as a research-backed starting cohort, not the finished 120-name list.** It is the seed you
run the network-expansion method (below) against.

## The method that actually worked — reuse it

Hashtag sweeps were the weakest way in. **Substack recommendation pages are a peer-curated network
graph**, and they are public. One fetch of Rory Johnston's `/recommendations` returned 25 names that a
respected oil analyst personally vouches for — higher signal than any hashtag, already filtered for
quality by someone in the field.

**Run this yourself, breadth-first:**

1. Take any Tier A name below.
2. Fetch `<their-substack>.com/recommendations`.
3. Add every commodity-relevant name to the list.
4. Repeat on the new names.

Two or three rounds gets you well past 120, and every name arrives with an implicit endorsement.
The same trick works on X/Twitter follow lists and newsletter "who I read" pages.

Won't co-create with you, but shape the vocabulary and are worth monitoring. S&P Global Commodity

## Gap found: metals is structurally different

**Searching metals repeatedly returned mining *equity* newsletters** — Metals and Miners, Mining Stock
Analyst, Don Durrett's newsletter, the Metals Investor Forum writers. These pick junior miners and gold
stocks. They care about companies, drill results and share prices, not curve shape, roll yield or COT
positioning.

**They are a poor fit and you should not spend Stage 2 warm-up on them.** The independent
metals-*futures* commentator — the copper-curve equivalent of Rory Johnston — did not surface in this
research. That is either a genuine white space or a cohort that lives somewhere other than public
newsletters.

Either way, two implications:

1. **Do not force metals into the seed cohort.** Weight the first 25 gives toward energy and ag, where
   the fit is proven.
2. **This is a content opportunity.** If nobody is publishing good public copper/precious-metals
   positioning analysis, the Friday COT chart for metals has no incumbent. Consider publishing it
   yourself under Stage 0 rather than waiting for a creator to hand it to.
